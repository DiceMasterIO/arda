# Architecture

> Evidence consolidated on September 25: the [study](features/2026-09-23-terrain-corrections/STUDY.md) and [experiment catalogue](features/2026-09-23-terrain-corrections/EXPERIMENTS.md) replace the raw terrain-corrections archive. Historical filenames below identify removed experiments; current evidence links lead to their retained summaries. Original/final worlds and the 32K output remain under `out/terrain-delivery/`.


The opt-in `orchestrator::fine_source` stage creates one full-world integer spectral field, composes macro terrain at fixed physical coordinates, and streams a versioned fine file. It admits the padded FFT peak and file size before allocation, includes nominal closed bounds and prepared fringe, and retains no extra complete composed raster. The reference calibration uses the native seed path; source identity uses the full world seed/attempt RNG stream. The production fine profile (raw recipe 3) gives tectonic belts bounded peak/saddle variation and replaces the repeated flat rift floor with smooth saturation. It retains the qualified range-based spectral gain, including near-zero gain on low-gradient plains, to avoid turning small terrain corrugations into thousands of closed lakes. The recipe-2 source remains available for explicit replay.

`generate_world_from_fine_terrain` copies and verifies that field, derives the 1 km continent used for climate and coarse drainage, and samples the same physical coordinates for the complete 100 m prepared bed. It bypasses legacy detail and 160-step evolution; shared water solving and area publication then follow the existing pipeline. The fine file has an optional version-4 manifest descriptor and a budgeted `World::fine_terrain` loader. Source generation and consumption are separate sequential phases; each has explicit resource admission. The source file checksum proves integrity, while correspondence to the supplied seed/attempt remains the caller's provenance contract.

**Recipe 5 (current `--terrain fine`).** `arda-gen::formation` replaces the spectral source and valley carving (logic/02 §fine-formation). It evolves the macro surface through six whole-domain integer stream-power levels, from 1,250 m down to the canonical 39.0625 m lattice. It also forms drowned coasts, deltas, tectonic basins and glacial trough lakes, and guarantees drainage both on the fine lattice and on the 100 m sampled bed. The result is written by `orchestrator::fine_formation` into the same canonical fine file, so everything downstream is unchanged. Atlas renders recipe ≥ 5 through `atlas::formed`: multi-baseline light, landform, climate and vegetation materials, fine-contour coasts and lakes, and thalweg-snapped rivers (logic/04 §atlas-formed). Recipe 4 remains reproducible through `FineRecipe::Valleys`.

The default generation entry point still uses legacy preparation. The opt-in facade `generate_from_fine_source` and CLI `generate --terrain fine` now carve connected valleys into the raw recipe-3 canonical file, then derive the final 1 km continent, 100 m bed, climate, drainage and published recipe-4 field from that transformed source. Separate source/world limits and private staging account for simultaneous raw and carved files. After shared water solving, recipe-4 area publication fills the existing wetness, moisture, canopy and cover fields. Atlas export consumes the declared fine source through one checksum-verified reader per export and bounded physical area windows, with a separate sparse 1 km canonical context for broad sky exposure on recipe-4 worlds. The renderer evaluates fine land material and slopes, averages shaded fine samples for reduced pixels, and retains saved 100 m coast, lake and channel authority. Legacy worlds and recipe-2 fine worlds retain their previous source and lighting paths. Earlier recipe-1/2 small-world and full seed-42 qualification is historical evidence for that baseline, including its 32K export and remaining visual differences: [final delivery](features/2026-09-23-terrain-corrections/EXPERIMENTS.md#accepted-look-qualification), [complete-world evidence](features/2026-09-23-terrain-corrections/EXPERIMENTS.md#fine-world-integration).

## Composition

`main` parses clap once and dispatches each `Command` variant to its private handler; handlers build configuration or parse coordinates, load through `World`, and call only the facade (`crates/arda-cli/src/main.rs:271`). There is no DI container: dependencies are plain values, references, closures, and concrete memory/file stores (`crates/arda/src/export_quality.rs:20`, `crates/arda-render/src/overview/streaming.rs:30`).

The generator admits the full request before reserving a fresh output, generates and validates a continent, evolves the complete modeled terrain once, persists immutable prepared tiles, releases the dense terrain, runs one shared annual-water solve, builds its final index, then composes and writes each exported area (`crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:344`, `crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:357`, `crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:406`, `crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:422`, `crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:441`, `crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:450`). Global/coarse layers precede final manifest publication; the manifest rename is the completion stamp (`crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:464`, `crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:480`, `crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:535`).

Preparation, shared solving, and final area composition execute sequentially. Initial structural relief uses the existing dense coarse/uplift field for in-domain 5 km gate samples and the direct coarse sampler beyond the modeled rectangle; `boundary_height` applies the same absolute-coordinate rule (`crates/arda-gen/src/area/prepare.rs:51`, `crates/arda-gen/src/area/prepare.rs:104`, `crates/arda-gen/src/continent/bundles.rs:30`). Uplift, fractional MFD contributing area, creep and collapse cross publication-area boundaries; only the modeled outer rim remains fixed. No final area reads another final area file: physical neighbor data comes from the shared prepared domain, so crossings do not depend on output order (`crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:406`, `crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:450`). Dense terrain and water-stage memory/file work are admitted before publication; insufficient limits leave the requested output path untouched (`crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:332`, `crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator.rs:344`).

Admission accounts for dense terrain inputs and scratch at 53 bytes per modeled fine cell plus container headers on the current 64-bit layout, conservatively summed with water-stage reservations (`crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator/generation_limits.rs`). Default and MICRO requests fit the default 16 GiB allowance; a 4000×4000 km request requires larger explicit limits and is refused before output creation under defaults (`crates/arda-gen/src/orchestrator/generation_limits_tests.rs`). Candidate06 completed five-world validation with fewer repeated pond patterns; parallel drainage and large regional basin geometry remain recorded open, while candidate02 records the preceding implementation (`docs/capstone/changelog.md`, `docs/capstone/open-items.md`).

Quality exports compose the stored world with renderer-owned display resolution. `ImageQuality` validates one edge and derives an aspect-preserving overview size; area `Custom` scale maps that edge onto the unchanged 512×512 saved cell grid (`crates/arda-render/src/quality.rs:9`, `crates/arda-render/src/quality.rs:41`, `crates/arda-render/src/channels.rs:11`). Exact overview exports use 256-row output bands with up to 14 context rows above and below, and may reload an uncached area for each intersecting band. The world Atlas overview facade uses saved adjacent channel strips, terminal footprints and junction hulls. Older recipes retain display width `min(8 × saved_width_dm, 10_000 dm)` and full opacity; recipe 4 uses `min(3 × saved_width_dm, 4_000 dm)` and 13/16 opacity; recipe 5 uses `min(4 × saved_width_dm, 12_000 dm)`, full opacity and thalweg-snapped vertices. Q20 pixel coverage unions those shapes with bounded adaptive subdivision (depth 6 and 250 million work units per tile construction and row); sea and lake feature masks retain ownership. The older Mid/Dark chamfer symbols remain in the legacy `write_atlas_overview_png` and `OverviewRaster::push_atlas` APIs, while Classic keeps its right/down Dark extension (`crates/arda-render/src/overview/channel_overlay.rs:168`, `crates/arda-render/src/overview/channel_overlay.rs:287`, `crates/arda-render/src/channel_geometry.rs:439`, `crates/arda-render/src/overview/streaming.rs:51`). Legacy `export_overview` retains the full raster and remains the CLI path for Preview `--px` and a public library entry point (`crates/arda/src/lib.rs:146`, `crates/arda-cli/src/main.rs:191`).

Atlas's per-pixel sampler uses exact rational saved-cell centers when an area's output partition is at least 512 pixels along that axis, and the existing half-open box footprint when smaller. It filters samples to the owning terrain class, averages RGB palette and Q12 light separately, then modulates once; land alone receives directional relief, sea uses saved negative height, and lake depth comes from validated saved surfaces and bed heights, while channel authority stays in the existing rendering path (`crates/arda-render/src/atlas.rs:302`, `crates/arda-render/src/atlas.rs:418`, `crates/arda-render/src/overview.rs:428`, `crates/arda-render/src/channels.rs:448`). Atlas and Classic share the bounded 256-row overview pipeline, including unequal area partitions and possible rereads across bands (`crates/arda-render/src/overview/streaming.rs:55`, `crates/arda-render/src/overview/streaming.rs:97`).

Older Atlas worlds tint the elevation palette from saved wetness using Q12 weight `wetness×2048/(wetness+12)` toward RGB `[81,126,73]`. Recipe-4 worlds instead use saved climate moisture and canopy, independent of angular drainage ownership. Both paths derive exposed-rock colour from the saved-height gradient, blend rock tint continuously from 1–2 km elevation, and add a slope-weighted pale snow appearance from 2.85–4.3 km. The northwest light uses stronger bounded Q12 modulation. These are deterministic display rules, not simulated lithology, vegetation or snow storage; they use the existing fixed halo and palette/light grids (`crates/arda-render/src/atlas.rs`).

For Linear×Linear output, Atlas reconstructs displayed land/sea ownership from four class-directed signed heights using exact rational pixel centers and i128 bilinear weights. Land samples are at least +1 mm and sea samples at most −1 mm. Positive chooses land, negative sea, and exact zero retains saved ownership. Saved lakes, quads touching lakes, alternating land/sea checkerboards, exact saved-cell centers and guarded one-cell islands/straits retain saved ownership. Any Box axis also retains the existing aggregation/ownership rule. Area colour and channel clipping and both overview paths use this same classifier; an internal AtlasSea feature prevents connected channel overlay and legacy river-trunk widening over reconstructed sea. Classic remains unchanged (`crates/arda-render/src/atlas.rs:412`, `crates/arda-render/src/channels.rs:451`, `crates/arda-render/src/overview.rs:428`, `crates/arda-render/src/overview/streaming.rs:92`).

## Frontend

No human-facing frontend is implemented; the current surfaces are the CLI and Rust API, and PNG/JSON files are export artifacts (`crates/arda-cli/src/main.rs:12`, `crates/arda/src/lib.rs:1`).
